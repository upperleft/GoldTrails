# Gold Trails: Canadian research dossiers

Checked October 18, 2026. Research preview only; these dossiers have not been imported into the website. “Source checked” means the cited passage was inspected, not that an agency approved a visit.

## Reading the evidence

Documented findings, evidence limits and planning interpretations are labelled separately. All historical numbers retain their geographic scope and period. Seasonal planning notes are interpretations; local station and gauge analysis remains outstanding. No jurisdiction-wide panning size or opportunity rating is invented. Existing creator profiles and Canadian map locations are preserved.

### Shared season method

Use 1991–2020 local climate normals for historical context and WaterOffice station/map searches for current water level and discharge. Match the station to the intended locality and watershed. These tools do not establish safe wading thresholds, legal access, operational approval or a guaranteed best month. [Canadian Climate Normals](https://climate.weather.gc.ca/climate_normals/index_e.html); [Real-Time Hydrometric Data](https://wateroffice.ec.gc.ca/mainmenu/real_time_data_index_e.html).

## British Columbia

### Gold geology and size

- **Documented:** Gold occurs in lode systems including porphyry, vein and skarn deposits, as well as placers; these require different research and recovery methods. [Gold in British Columbia — Information Circular 2015-6](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/mineral-exploration-mining/documents/mineral-development-office/gold_september_2015.pdf?v=082710) — PDF p. 2, deposit types.

- **Research gap:** Typical placer particle sizes must be documented by creek or district; no province-wide nugget expectation is supported.

### Production evidence

- **Documented:** Approximate historical lode gold production: 32,000,000 ounces; 1858–2013. Approximate brochure total; do not combine with annual figures. [Gold in British Columbia — Information Circular 2015-6](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/mineral-exploration-mining/documents/mineral-development-office/gold_september_2015.pdf?v=082710) — PDF p. 1, introductory production statement.

- **Documented:** Approximate historical placer gold production: 6,000,000 ounces; 1858–2013. Separate from lode output; not a current reserve estimate. [Gold in British Columbia — Information Circular 2015-6](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/mineral-exploration-mining/documents/mineral-development-office/gold_september_2015.pdf?v=082710) — PDF p. 1, introductory production statement.

- **Documented:** Annual mine gold production: 17.6 tonnes; 2024. Preliminary; includes industrial production, not recreational returns. [Gold facts](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/minerals-mining/mining-data-statistics-analysis/minerals-metals-facts/gold-facts) — Canadian gold production by region, 2024(p).

### Seasons and water

- **Documented:** The province warns against spring freshet, early-summer high water and significant rain; check wildfire closures and road conditions before travelling. [Recreational hand panning](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/mineral-exploration-mining/mineral-titles/recreational-hand-panning) — Safety tips.

- **Planning interpretation — review before publication:** Compare coastal, interior and mountain localities separately. A single provincial season would conceal important differences in water conditions and elevation. [Canadian Climate Normals](https://climate.weather.gc.ca/climate_normals/index_e.html) — 1991–2020 normals introduction and location/province searches.

- **Research gap:** No evidence-backed jurisdiction-wide best-month range established. Select a locality, representative climate station and relevant hydrometric gauge; check current closures and conditions.

### Land access and rules

- **Documented:** Limited recreational hand panning uses a pan and hand shovel, without a Free Miner Certificate. Sluices, dredges and metal detectors are outside this permission. Obtain titleholder and private-owner consent where required; parks and other excluded lands are not opened by this rule. [Recreational hand panning](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/mineral-exploration-mining/mineral-titles/recreational-hand-panning) — What is recreational hand panning?; Where can I hand pan?.

### Associated minerals

- **Documented:** Gold is associated with copper-bearing porphyry systems and several vein and sulphide deposit types; a geological association does not establish recoverable stream gold. [Gold in British Columbia — Information Circular 2015-6](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/mineral-exploration-mining/documents/mineral-development-office/gold_september_2015.pdf?v=082710) — PDF p. 2, Gold Deposit Types.

### Discoveries and mining history

- **Documented:** Historic camps include Cariboo–Barkerville, Bridge River, Rossland and Hedley. Their mining history is not evidence of open recreational access. [Gold in British Columbia — Information Circular 2015-6](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/mineral-exploration-mining/documents/mineral-development-office/gold_september_2015.pdf?v=082710) — PDF pp. 1–2, camps and map.

- **Research gap:** Verify first-discovery chronology and named Indigenous contributors against district-specific historical records.

### Maps and research resources

- **Documented:** Use the official panning-reserve maps and current mineral-title information to investigate a specific site; a reserve listing does not waive other restrictions. [Recreational hand panning](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/mineral-exploration-mining/mineral-titles/recreational-hand-panning) — Recreational panning reserves.

**Report card:** unscored pending representative recreational evidence. Unknown is not a one-star rating.

## Yukon

### Gold geology and size

- **Documented:** Yukon placers include high terraces, unglaciated valley fills, interglacial deposits, glacially reworked material and recent alluvium; glacial history changes where older gravels survive. [Overview of Yukon placer geology, gold production, and prospects](https://open.yukon.ca/information/overview-of-yukon-placer-geology-gold-production-and-prospects) — Official abstract, five placer classes.

- **Documented:** Sulphur Creek records describe both fine gold and small nuggets, with differences among cuts and years. This historical industrial example cannot predict a visitor’s pan. [Yukon Placer Database: Sulphur Gold Placers, Sulphur Creek](https://ygsftp.gov.yk.ca/ygsftp/publications/placer_atlas/sulphur_gold_placers_sulphur_creek.pdf) — PDF p. 4, Gold Comments.

### Production evidence

- **Documented:** Annual mine gold production: 6 tonnes; 2024. Preliminary; not separated here into lode and placer. [Gold facts](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/minerals-mining/mining-data-statistics-analysis/minerals-metals-facts/gold-facts) — Canadian gold production by region, 2024(p).

- **Research gap:** An overview quotes more than 15 million crude ounces since 1886 without a verified ending year; withhold that cumulative statistic until the underlying report is identified.

### Seasons and water

- **Planning interpretation — review before publication:** Choose the district first and verify thaw, snow, river levels and travel logistics locally. Historical mining seasons do not establish a safe recreational window. [Canadian Climate Normals](https://climate.weather.gc.ca/climate_normals/index_e.html) — 1991–2020 normals introduction and location/province searches.

- **Research gap:** No evidence-backed jurisdiction-wide best-month range established. Select a locality, representative climate station and relevant hydrometric gauge; check current closures and conditions.

### Land access and rules

- **Documented:** Recreational panning is limited to a pan and shovel on public land that is neither withdrawn nor staked. Ask the Mining Recorder where it is permitted; historic objects, fossils and bones must be left and reported as directed. [Pan for gold recreationally](https://yukon.ca/en/outdoor-recreation-and-wildlife/trails-tours-and-activities/pan-gold-recreationally) — Where you can pan; What you can use; historical objects.

### Associated minerals

- **Documented:** A historical Sulphur Creek operation record describes gold attached to quartz and schist-rich gravels; these are local observations, not universal Yukon indicators. [Yukon Placer Database: Sulphur Gold Placers, Sulphur Creek](https://ygsftp.gov.yk.ca/ygsftp/publications/placer_atlas/sulphur_gold_placers_sulphur_creek.pdf) — PDF p. 4, Gold Comments and Surficial Geology.

### Discoveries and mining history

- **Documented:** The 1896 Bonanza Creek discovery triggered the Klondike rush. Parks Canada credits Keish / Skookum Jim Mason and identifies Káa Goox / Dawson Charlie, Shaaw Tláa / Kate and George Carmack in the discovery party. [Discovery Claim National Historic Site](https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/yt/klondike/culture/lhn-hns-disc) — Background and milestones.

- **Research gap:** The Parks Canada page gives August 16 in its formal significance/milestones but August 17 in a second narrative; use 1896 until the day-level discrepancy is resolved.

### Maps and research resources

- **Documented:** The survey overview organizes ten historical placer regions, including Klondike, Indian River, Mayo, Kluane and Livingstone, to guide further district research. [Overview of Yukon placer geology, gold production, and prospects](https://open.yukon.ca/information/overview-of-yukon-placer-geology-gold-production-and-prospects) — Official abstract, historical regions.

**Report card:** unscored pending representative recreational evidence. Unknown is not a one-star rating.

## Alberta

### Gold geology and size

- **Documented:** Placer gold is documented in river gravels; the AGS atlas describes Recent and Pleistocene placer occurrences in the western sedimentary basin. [Chapter 34: Mineral Resources of the Western Canada Sedimentary Basin](https://ags.aer.ca/publications/atlas-western-canada-sedimentary-basin/chapter-34-mineral-resources) — Metallic Minerals — Precious Metals.

- **Documented:** In Edmonton’s North Saskatchewan River valley, the museum describes flour-gold flakes often less than half a millimetre across. This locality-specific description is not a measured average for every Alberta river. [Buried Treasure — panning for gold in Edmonton’s river valley](https://www.royalalbertamuseum.ca/blog/buried-treasure-panning-gold-edmontons-river-valley) — Opening geology discussion, flour gold.

### Production evidence

- **Documented:** AGS describes Alberta gold recovery as a by-product of sand and gravel processing, alongside historical alluvial recovery. This does not measure a hobbyist’s likely recovery. [Mineral production](https://ags.aer.ca/our-science/mineral-resources/mineral-production) — Gold / metallic mineral production discussion.

- **Research gap:** No cumulative provincial gold total with a defensible period and scope established.

### Seasons and water

- **Documented:** The toolkit specifies May 15–September 15 for placer operations on Class A/B waters and year-round operations on Class C/D waters, subject to restrictions. This is an operating rule, not a blanket hand-panning season or a safety forecast. [Minerals placer mining tool kit](https://www.alberta.ca/minerals-placer-mining-tool-kit) — Maps — Water body maps.

- **Planning interpretation — review before publication:** Treat legal operating dates separately from snowmelt, flood conditions and practical access. Choose a local climate station and stream gauge before recommending months. [Canadian Climate Normals](https://climate.weather.gc.ca/climate_normals/index_e.html) — 1991–2020 normals introduction and location/province searches.

- **Research gap:** No evidence-backed jurisdiction-wide best-month range established. Select a locality, representative climate station and relevant hydrometric gauge; check current closures and conditions.

### Land access and rules

- **Documented:** Hand-held nonmechanized recreational panning needs no mineral agreement or licence. A sluicebox or other mechanized placer equipment requires a placer minerals licence. River and stream dredging is prohibited; occupied-land crossings require permission. [Minerals placer mining tool kit](https://www.alberta.ca/minerals-placer-mining-tool-kit) — Activities — Find out when and where; Sluice for gold.

### Associated minerals

- **Documented:** The museum reports platinum flakes with North Saskatchewan River placer gold in smaller amounts. This association is local evidence, not a promise that every black-sand concentrate contains precious metals. [Buried Treasure — panning for gold in Edmonton’s river valley](https://www.royalalbertamuseum.ca/blog/buried-treasure-panning-gold-edmontons-river-valley) — Opening geology discussion, placer deposits.

- **Partly resolved:** Verify an Alberta-specific heavy-mineral assemblage and particle-size study; do not turn generic black sands into a local gold guarantee. North Saskatchewan platinum association and Edmonton flour-gold dimensions now sourced; a complete heavy-mineral assemblage is still missing.

### Discoveries and mining history

- **Evidence limit:** The atlas documents historic small-scale placer production in the basin but does not establish Alberta’s first discovery or a province-wide historical total. [Chapter 34: Mineral Resources of the Western Canada Sedimentary Basin](https://ags.aer.ca/publications/atlas-western-canada-sedimentary-basin/chapter-34-mineral-resources) — Precious Metals.

- **Documented:** James Hector reported North Saskatchewan River gold in 1859 after being shown samples from near Fort Edmonton. The museum describes a later dredging boom and the subsequent decline as miners headed to the Klondike. [Buried Treasure — panning for gold in Edmonton’s river valley](https://www.royalalbertamuseum.ca/blog/buried-treasure-panning-gold-edmontons-river-valley) — Historic account, 1859 and 1890s.

- **Partly resolved:** First-discovery names and dates need a directly inspected historical source. 1859 report near Fort Edmonton documented by Royal Alberta Museum; not asserted as the first human discovery in Alberta.

### Maps and research resources

- **Documented:** The toolkit links mineral dispositions, classified water-body maps and Alberta River Basins advisories for site-specific planning. [Minerals placer mining tool kit](https://www.alberta.ca/minerals-placer-mining-tool-kit) — Maps.

**Report card:** unscored pending representative recreational evidence. Unknown is not a one-star rating.

## Saskatchewan

### Gold geology and size

- **Documented:** The provincial fact sheet identifies fault-hosted orogenic gold in the Canadian Shield and gold associated with Flin Flon–Creighton volcanogenic massive-sulphide deposits. [Gold in Saskatchewan — fact sheet](https://pubsaskdev.blob.core.windows.net/pubsask-prod/139031/ER%252BFact%252BSheet%252B-%252BGold.pdf) — PDF p. 1, Deposit Type.

- **Documented:** The AGS basin atlas documents Recent and Pleistocene placer gravels in Saskatchewan, alongside Alberta and the Northwest Territories; it says the bedrock sources of these particular placers were unknown. [Chapter 34: Mineral Resources of the Western Canada Sedimentary Basin](https://ags.aer.ca/publications/atlas-western-canada-sedimentary-basin/chapter-34-mineral-resources) — Precious Metals.

- **Research gap:** No province-wide typical placer size verified; northern bedrock ore must not be represented as ordinary river-pan gold.

### Production evidence

- **Evidence limit:** The gold fact sheet reports substantial Seabee production since 1991 and Flin Flon by-product gold, but its cumulative cutoff is not explicit; these figures are withheld from structured statistics. [Gold in Saskatchewan — fact sheet](https://pubsaskdev.blob.core.windows.net/pubsask-prod/139031/ER%252BFact%252BSheet%252B-%252BGold.pdf) — PDF p. 1, Summary.

- **Research gap:** Resolve historical production cutoffs before adding cumulative statistics; no current province-wide total is asserted.

### Seasons and water

- **Planning interpretation — review before publication:** Separate the northern Shield from prairie rivers when assessing travel and climate. The pan-only equipment rule still applies during suitable weather. [Canadian Climate Normals](https://climate.weather.gc.ca/climate_normals/index_e.html) — 1991–2020 normals introduction and location/province searches.

- **Research gap:** No evidence-backed jurisdiction-wide best-month range established. Select a locality, representative climate station and relevant hydrometric gauge; check current closures and conditions.

### Land access and rules

- **Documented:** Recreational testing is pan-only: no shovel, classifier, screen or sluice. Obtain surface-owner permission and authorization for third-party mineral rights. Other equipment needs a claim and applicable approvals; do not damage the bed, bank or boundary of a waterbody. [Recreational Gold Panning and Placer Mining](https://www.saskatchewan.ca/business/agriculture-natural-resources-and-industry/mineral-exploration-and-mining/mineral-tenure/recreational-gold-panning-and-placer-mining) — Recreational gold panning requirements.

### Associated minerals

- **Documented:** At Seabee, shear-hosted quartz-tourmaline veins and biotite alteration accompany gold mineralization; the deposit record lists pyrite and pyrrhotite among host minerals. This is a bedrock example. [Mineral Deposit Query: SMDI 0382, Seabee Mine / Seabee Gold Operation](https://mineraldeposits.saskatchewan.ca/Home/Viewdetails/0382) — Geological Details; Host Minerals.

### Discoveries and mining history

- **Documented:** The dated deposit history records Seabee operation production beginning in 1991. The original Seabee deposit and the wider operation using Santoy ore must be distinguished. [Mineral Deposit Query: SMDI 0382, Seabee Mine / Seabee Gold Operation](https://mineraldeposits.saskatchewan.ca/Home/Viewdetails/0382) — Exploration History entries dated December 1, 2023/2024.

### Maps and research resources

- **Documented:** Use Saskatchewan’s deposit database for geology, dated histories and underlying assessment references; deposit coordinates do not confer collecting permission. [Mineral Deposit Query: SMDI 0382, Seabee Mine / Seabee Gold Operation](https://mineraldeposits.saskatchewan.ca/Home/Viewdetails/0382) — SMDI 0382 and linked deposit query.

- **Documented:** Check MARS tenure and the province’s panning instructions before choosing land or equipment. [Recreational Gold Panning and Placer Mining](https://www.saskatchewan.ca/business/agriculture-natural-resources-and-industry/mineral-exploration-and-mining/mineral-tenure/recreational-gold-panning-and-placer-mining) — Recreational panning / MARS references.

**Report card:** unscored pending representative recreational evidence. Unknown is not a one-star rating.

## Ontario

### Gold geology and size

- **Documented:** Timmins gold occurs in ancient volcanic and sedimentary Shield rocks, with gold-bearing quartz veins related to the Porcupine–Destor fault system; glacial deposits cover much of the bedrock. [Timmins GeoTour](https://files.ontario.ca/ndmnrf-geotours-3/ndmnrf-geotours-timmins-en-2021-12-13.pdf) — PDF pp. 4–5, Timmins geology.

- **Research gap:** District-specific recreational placer occurrence and size evidence remains incomplete; Timmins lode production cannot supply those fields.

### Production evidence

- **Documented:** Gold output reported for more than 50 Timmins mines: 70,000,000 ounces; century following 1909. Historical camp figure, not an Ontario total or a precisely dated current cumulative total. [Timmins GeoTour](https://files.ontario.ca/ndmnrf-geotours-3/ndmnrf-geotours-timmins-en-2021-12-13.pdf) — PDF p. 3, opening history.

- **Documented:** Annual mine gold production: 79.9 tonnes; 2024. Preliminary; not a measure of recreational placer opportunity. [Gold facts](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/minerals-mining/mining-data-statistics-analysis/minerals-metals-facts/gold-facts) — Canadian gold production by region, 2024(p).

### Seasons and water

- **Planning interpretation — review before publication:** Compare southern and northern localities separately. Check local snow, ice, flow and access rather than assign the same months to the whole province. [Canadian Climate Normals](https://climate.weather.gc.ca/climate_normals/index_e.html) — 1991–2020 normals introduction and location/province searches.

- **Research gap:** No evidence-backed jurisdiction-wide best-month range established. Select a locality, representative climate station and relevant hydrometric gauge; check current closures and conditions.

### Land access and rules

- **Documented:** Ontario’s hobby policy describes conditional enforcement discretion for small personal collections with hand tools, including pans. It is not a blanket exemption. Check mining and surface rights, consent, withdrawals and protected areas; other laws still apply. [Hobby Mineral Collecting Policy](https://ero.ontario.ca/public/2023-08/Hobby%20Mineral%20Collecting%20Policy%20Final.pdf) — PDF pp. 3–6, scope, thresholds, land categories and conditions.

### Associated minerals

- **Documented:** Quartz veins are an important Timmins ore setting, but quartz alone is not proof of gold and a bedrock ore photograph is not a typical placer sample. [Timmins GeoTour](https://files.ontario.ca/ndmnrf-geotours-3/ndmnrf-geotours-timmins-en-2021-12-13.pdf) — PDF pp. 3–5, quartz veins.

### Discoveries and mining history

- **Documented:** The GeoTour describes the 1909 Big Dome discovery by Harry Preston and Jack Wilson, followed by major Hollinger and McIntyre developments. [Timmins GeoTour](https://files.ontario.ca/ndmnrf-geotours-3/ndmnrf-geotours-timmins-en-2021-12-13.pdf) — PDF p. 3, Timmins mining history.

### Maps and research resources

- **Documented:** Use the policy’s MLAS and land-status resources before collecting; a mineral occurrence does not establish a right to enter. [Hobby Mineral Collecting Policy](https://ero.ontario.ca/public/2023-08/Hobby%20Mineral%20Collecting%20Policy%20Final.pdf) — PDF p. 7, resources.

**Report card:** unscored pending representative recreational evidence. Unknown is not a one-star rating.

## Québec

### Gold geology and size

- **Documented:** The Amos bulletin documents Abitibi orogenic quartz-carbonate gold veins in the Bartec and Carpentier zones, plus distinct intrusion-associated styles. This regional example is not a model for every Québec gold occurrence. [Geology of the Amos area — BG 2020-06](https://gq.mines.gouv.qc.ca/bulletins-geologiques_en/amos_en/) — Economic Geology — Bartec and Carpentier prospective zones.

- **Documented:** Val-d’Or–Senneterre Quaternary mapping documents substantial surficial cover. Glacial sediments and transported stones must be considered separately from underlying bedrock. [Géologie des dépôts de surface: Val-d’Or–Senneterre — BQ 2022-02](https://gq.mines.gouv.qc.ca/bulletins-quaternaires/bq-2022-02-abitibi-phase-1/) — Surficial geology / regional setting.

- **Research gap:** Verify locality-specific placer size rather than infer it from Abitibi ore or Beauce anecdotes.

### Production evidence

- **Documented:** Annual mine gold production: 55.5 tonnes; 2024. Preliminary; not a placer or cumulative historical total. [Gold facts](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/minerals-mining/mining-data-statistics-analysis/minerals-metals-facts/gold-facts) — Canadian gold production by region, 2024(p).

- **Research gap:** Do not assign the cross-border Abitibi belt’s historical production wholly to Québec; no provincial historical total is entered.

### Seasons and water

- **Planning interpretation — review before publication:** Select a locality before describing the season; the southern gold districts and northern Québec cannot share a useful single weather window. [Canadian Climate Normals](https://climate.weather.gc.ca/climate_normals/index_e.html) — 1991–2020 normals introduction and location/province searches.

- **Research gap:** No evidence-backed jurisdiction-wide best-month range established. Select a locality, representative climate station and relevant hydrometric gauge; check current closures and conditions.

### Land access and rules

- **Documented:** The official guidance distinguishes very superficial prospection from work requiring a DEE, the exclusive exploration right formerly called a claim. Its no-permit wording for limited prospection must not be treated as permission to disturb streambeds or enter any property. [Obtenir un droit exclusif d’exploration (DEE)](https://www.quebec.ca/agriculture-environnement-et-ressources-naturelles/mines/realiser-projet-minier/titres/droit-exclusif-exploration/obtenir) — Prospection; droit exclusif d’exploration.

- **Research gap:** Confirm panning-specific environmental requirements and 2025 changes for private/urban land, including exceptions. Superficial-prospecting guidance alone is insufficient for a visitor recommendation.

### Associated minerals

- **Documented:** Quartz-carbonate gangue and sheared volcanic or intrusive rocks help describe some Amos-area gold settings; these associations require local mapping and sampling, not visual assumptions. [Geology of the Amos area — BG 2020-06](https://gq.mines.gouv.qc.ca/bulletins-geologiques_en/amos_en/) — Economic Geology.

### Discoveries and mining history

- **Research gap:** Beauce 1835 discovery / 1847 production chronology was found in the MRNF search index, but the original page could not be retrieved. Hold dates until a directly inspectable primary record is obtained.

### Maps and research resources

- **Documented:** Géologie Québec bulletins link mapped outcrops, analyses and SIGEOM mineralized-site records; use their original scales and regional coverage. [Geology of the Amos area — BG 2020-06](https://gq.mines.gouv.qc.ca/bulletins-geologiques_en/amos_en/) — Data and Analyses; Economic Geology.

- **Documented:** GESTIM is the official tenure resource identified by the DEE guidance. Verify current title and access conditions before planning fieldwork. [Obtenir un droit exclusif d’exploration (DEE)](https://www.quebec.ca/agriculture-environnement-et-ressources-naturelles/mines/realiser-projet-minier/titres/droit-exclusif-exploration/obtenir) — Obtaining a DEE / GESTIM.

**Report card:** unscored pending representative recreational evidence. Unknown is not a one-star rating.

## Manitoba

[Nopiming Provincial Park visitor guide](https://manitoba.ca/sd/pubs/parks-protected-spaces/park_info/nopiming_pp.pdf) — PDF pp. 6–7, gold rush and Central Manitoba Mine.

- **Research gap:** Typical placer sizes and well-supported recreational placer districts remain unverified.

### Production evidence

- **Evidence limit:** The Diana–Gem Lake inventory records gold production in 1928–1932, 1934–1938 and 1940–1941. Small differences between its narrative and table totals must be reconciled before importing a single cumulative number. [Mineral Inventory File 214: Diana–Gem Lake Mine](https://www.manitoba.ca/iem/min-ed/mbhistory/mininv/214.htm) — History of Exploration and Development; History of Production.

- **Research gap:** No provincial cumulative historical total with period and scope verified; missing does not mean zero.

### Seasons and water

- **Planning interpretation — review before publication:** Research the intended southern or northern locality, seasonal road access and flow conditions separately. Do not recommend old mine workings as panning sites. [Canadian Climate Normals](https://climate.weather.gc.ca/climate_normals/index_e.html) — 1991–2020 normals introduction and location/province searches.

- **Research gap:** No evidence-backed jurisdiction-wide best-month range established. Select a locality, representative climate station and relevant hydrometric gauge; check current closures and conditions.

### Land access and rules

- **Documented:** The Mines and Minerals Act establishes licensing and open-Crown-mineral-land conditions, subject to surface rights and restrictions. ### Associated minerals

- **Documented:** The Central Manitoba Mine worked gold-bearing quartz veins. This is a geological history example, not proof that loose quartz or nearby gravel contains gold. [Nopiming Provincial Park visitor guide](https://manitoba.ca/sd/pubs/parks-protected-spaces/park_info/nopiming_pp.pdf) — PDF p. 7, Central Manitoba Mine.

- **Documented:** Diana–Gem Lake’s historical inventory describes gold-bearing quartz with arsenopyrite, pyrite, galena and chalcopyrite in an Archean volcanic/intrusive setting. This is deposit-specific ore mineralogy. [Mineral Inventory File 214: Diana–Gem Lake Mine](https://www.manitoba.ca/iem/min-ed/mbhistory/mininv/214.htm) — Description of Deposit.

### Discoveries and mining history

- **Documented:** In 1911 Duncan Twohearts brought gold-bearing Rice Lake rock to Arthur Quesnel and Captain Pelletier, helping initiate the local rush. The guide’s park context does not authorize collecting. [Nopiming Provincial Park visitor guide](https://manitoba.ca/sd/pubs/parks-protected-spaces/park_info/nopiming_pp.pdf) — PDF p. 6, Rice Lake gold discovery.

- **Research gap:** Government historical guides differ over the recorded claim holder after the Twohearts discovery; do not settle that attribution without a claim record.
